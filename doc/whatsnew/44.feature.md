Undepreciated value is implemented. `landloss.loss.pricing.wall_udv_incl_gst_nzd`
and `beta_wall_udv_incl_gst_nzd` produce the
`retaining_wall_udv_incl_gst_nzd` that `settle` takes, which was the last thing
standing between a retaining wall and an end-to-end settlement.

It comes off the **same square-metre rate as the repair cost**, confirmed
against the costing spreadsheet, so the site allowance is the whole of the
difference between them:

```text
repair cost = m2 rate x wall face area x (1 + site multiplier)
udv         = m2 rate x wall face area
```

Access, earthworks and constructability are what it costs to work on a
particular site, not part of what the wall cost to build, so they have no place
in UDV — there is nowhere to pass them. Both figures are now one function with
the multiplier as its only variable, so they cannot drift apart.

Two properties are pinned by tests. **Repair cost is never below UDV**, checked
across all 27 site ratings; it holds because the multiplier is never negative,
which makes it a check on the arithmetic rather than evidence the gap is the
right size. And **no age or condition enters UDV** — undepreciated means no
deduction for age, so a twenty-year-old wall and a new one of the same
construction and size are worth the same, and `initial_condition` has no bearing
on it.

This corrects a misreading recorded earlier in `asset-pricing-approach.md`.
Chris Ewens's "set fees for under-depreciated value", and UDV being "without the
additional items the square-metre rates build into the Land SOW", do not mean a
separate rate table. They mean the wall rate alone, without the other Land SOW
lines beside it and without the site multiplier.

**L-34** narrows accordingly. The limitation is specification, not rates: one
rate for both assumes the replacement matches the wall that failed, where a
failed wall is often rebuilt to a more substantial current standard that would
want a higher rate on the repair side. The bias runs one way — settlement is
`min(repair, cap)` less the excess, so understating repair cost can only lower a
settlement — so the study under-reports NHC's liability rather than
over-reporting it.
